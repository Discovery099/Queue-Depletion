## What has been learned since generation 2,000

1. **The search fits its home folds.** Leaders' scores rose from 0.42 to 0.64 while their scores on unseen folds fell: retention
   43% at generation 15,008 and 18% at 20,008. More generations of the same search would produce more of the same.
2. **A small residual survives on unseen folds.** In the first exchange all 20 leaders kept a positive geometry-matched alpha
   (+3 to +8 points); the entries that kept the most were a failed break of the prior day's extreme inside the cloud (15 minutes,
   profit factor 1.17 to 1.23 away) and price crossing its average (30 minutes, 1.19 away). Generation 17196 is the only genome to
   pass the home screen; on all six folds it has 63.8% wins, profit factor 1.30, alpha +6.5.
3. **Frequency and quality trade off.** Above 200 trades a year per instrument the best home profit factor at 65 to 80% wins is
   about 1.4 to 1.5; profit factors above 1.8 occur only below about 100 trades a year. No single entry gives both.
4. **Parameter refinement was blocked for part of the run** (errata item 8) and behavioural duplicates crowded the leaders
   (item 10). Both are fixed.

## What I misjudged

At generation 2,000 I ranked the hardened average cross first. It became a leader on its home folds and lost a third of its
profit factor away. I underweighted how fast selection on three folds overfits.

## Changes made (all tightenings, `reports/gate_changes.md`)

Symmetric overfit penalty in the score; in-sample record must be profitable and excludes the other island's folds; behavioural
duplicates kept out of the archive; island exchange every 2,500 generations with demotion of leaders that do not hold up.

## Directions that look exhausted

further parameter tuning of the current leaders.
